How to Tell When Bread Is Done Baking

Internal temperature, color and the hollow-thump test.

By BreadScopeCraft Kitchen·Updated Nov 24, 2026·5 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

The most reliable test is internal temperature: about 190–200°F (88–93°C) for lean crusty breads and about 190°F (88°C) for soft, enriched breads. A deep golden crust and a hollow sound when tapped on the bottom are good supporting signs.

In this guide
  1. 01  Use a thermometer
  2. 02  Color and sound
  3. 03  Let it cool
STEP / 01

Use a thermometer

Insert an instant-read thermometer into the center of the loaf from the side or bottom.

Lean breads (flour, water, salt, yeast) are done around 200–210°F (93–99°C); breads with milk, butter, eggs or sugar are done around 190°F (88°C).

STEP / 02

Color and sound

Crusty breads should be deep golden to brown — pale loaves often taste flat and go soft quickly.

Turn the loaf over and tap the bottom. A hollow sound suggests it’s baked through, though this test is less reliable than a thermometer.

STEP / 03

Let it cool

Bread keeps cooking as it cools, and the crumb is still setting. Cutting too soon can make it gummy.

Wait at least 45 minutes for rolls and small loaves, and 1–2 hours for large crusty loaves.

BAKER’S TIP

If the crust is browning too fast before the inside is done, lay a sheet of foil loosely over the top for the rest of the bake.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my bread gummy inside?

It was likely underbaked or sliced while hot. Check the internal temperature next time and let it cool fully.

Do I need a special thermometer?

Any instant-read kitchen thermometer works.

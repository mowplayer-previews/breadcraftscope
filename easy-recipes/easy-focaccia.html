Easy Olive Oil Focaccia

Crisp edges, an airy center and almost no kneading.

By BreadScopeCraft Kitchen·Updated Nov 24, 2026·Jump to recipe ↓
Makes1 pan, 9×13 inch (23×33 cm), about 12 pieces
Prep15 min
Rise2 ½–3 hours (or overnight in the fridge)
Bake22–28 min
Oven425°F (220°C)
WHY IT WORKS

A very wet dough strengthened with a few stretch-and-folds, rested in an oiled pan and dimpled before baking. Olive oil gives the crisp, golden bottom.

ADVERTISEMENT

Ingredients

  • 500 gbread flour (or all-purpose)
  • 10 gfine salt
  • 4 ginstant yeast
  • 400 gwarm water
  • 50 golive oil, divided
  • flaky salt and rosemary, for topping

Method

  1. Mix the flour, salt and yeast. Add the water and stir until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest 30 minutes.
  2. With wet hands, do a set of stretch-and-folds: lift one side of the dough, fold it over, turn the bowl and repeat 4 times. Repeat every 30 minutes, 3 times total.
  3. Pour half the olive oil into a 9×13-inch pan. Transfer the dough, turn it to coat, and let rise uncovered-but-draped 1–1½ hours, until puffy and nearly filling the pan.
  4. Preheat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Drizzle the remaining oil on top, dimple deeply with oiled fingers, and sprinkle with flaky salt and rosemary.
  5. Bake 22–28 minutes, until deep golden on top and crisp underneath. Cool a few minutes in the pan, then move to a rack.
BAKER’S TIP

For more flavor, refrigerate the dough in the oiled pan overnight, then let it sit at room temperature 1–2 hours before dimpling.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my dough so sticky?

Focaccia dough is meant to be very wet. Wet hands make it much easier to handle.

What toppings work?

Cherry tomatoes, olives, sliced onions or garlic — press them in after dimpling.

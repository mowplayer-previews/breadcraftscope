How to Store Homemade Bread So It Stays Fresh

Counter, bag, box or freezer — and why the fridge isn’t one of them.

By BreadCraftScope Kitchen·Updated Nov 24, 2026·6 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

Store crusty bread cut-side down on a board or in a paper bag for a day or two, and soft bread in a plastic bag. For anything longer, slice and freeze. The fridge makes bread go stale faster.

In this guide
  1. 01  Why bread goes stale
  2. 02  Crusty vs. soft breads
  3. 03  Reviving bread
STEP / 01

Why bread goes stale

Staling is mostly starch recrystallizing, not drying out. It happens fastest at refrigerator temperatures.

That’s why bread in the fridge feels stale sooner, even though it may resist mold longer.

STEP / 02

Crusty vs. soft breads

Crusty loaves keep their crust best uncovered cut-side down or in paper for 1–2 days. A plastic bag softens the crust.

Soft sandwich bread and rolls stay soft in a sealed plastic bag for 2–4 days.

STEP / 03

Reviving bread

Run a stale crusty loaf briefly under the tap and warm it at 350°F (175°C) for 5–10 minutes. The crust crisps and the inside softens.

Very stale bread is perfect for croutons, breadcrumbs or bread pudding.

BAKER’S TIP

A bread box gives a nice balance: enough humidity to slow staling, enough airflow to protect the crust.

Frequently asked questions

Should I store bread in the fridge?

Generally no, unless your kitchen is very warm and humid and mold is a problem.

How long does homemade bread last?

About 2–4 days at room temperature, depending on the bread, and up to 3 months frozen.

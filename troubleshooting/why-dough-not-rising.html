Why Isn’t My Dough Rising?

Temperature, yeast and other usual suspects.

By BreadScopeCraft Kitchen·Updated Nov 24, 2026·6 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

Dough that won’t rise is most often too cold, made with inactive yeast, or mixed with water that was too hot. Patience and a warmer spot fix most cases.

In this guide
  1. 01  It’s too cold
  2. 02  The yeast is old or was killed
  3. 03  Other causes
STEP / 01

It’s too cold

Below about 70°F (21°C), yeast works slowly. The dough may just need more time.

Move it to a warmer spot, such as an oven with only the light on.

ADVERTISEMENT
STEP / 02

The yeast is old or was killed

Expired or badly stored yeast loses strength. Test it in warm water with a little sugar.

Water above about 130°F (55°C) can kill yeast. Use water that feels warm, not hot, on your wrist.

STEP / 03

Other causes

Too much salt or sugar slows yeast, as does a very stiff dough.

Whole grain and rye doughs rise less dramatically by nature — look for puffiness, not doubling.

BAKER’S TIP

Store opened yeast in an airtight container in the fridge or freezer to keep it active for months.

Frequently asked questions

Can I save dough that didn’t rise?

If the yeast was dead, knead in fresh yeast dissolved in a little warm water and let it rise again.

How long is too long to wait?

If there’s no change at all after 2–3 hours in a warm spot, the yeast is likely the problem.

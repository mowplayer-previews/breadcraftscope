How to Bake Crusty Bread Without a Dutch Oven

Steam is the secret, and you can create it at home.

By BreadCraftScope Kitchen·Updated Nov 24, 2026·6 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

A covered Dutch oven traps steam from the dough, which keeps the crust soft long enough for the loaf to expand. You can get a similar effect with a hot baking sheet or stone and a pan of hot water or ice cubes for steam.

In this guide
  1. 01  Why steam matters
  2. 02  The baking-sheet method
  3. 03  Other options
STEP / 01

Why steam matters

In the first minutes of baking, a moist surface stays flexible, letting the loaf rise fully (oven spring).

Steam also helps the crust turn glossy and crackly.

ADVERTISEMENT
STEP / 02

The baking-sheet method

Place a heavy baking sheet or pizza stone on the middle rack and a sturdy metal pan on the rack below. Preheat to 450–475°F (230–245°C).

Slide the loaf onto the hot sheet and immediately pour about 1 cup of boiling water into the lower pan. Close the door quickly.

STEP / 03

Other options

An upside-down deep roasting pan or a large oven-safe bowl over the loaf works as a makeshift cover.

Remove the steam source or cover after about 20 minutes so the crust can brown and crisp.

BAKER’S TIP

Protect your oven window: never splash water on hot glass, and use a towel when pouring.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a glass baking dish?

Avoid pouring water into glass or ceramic at high heat — it can crack. Use metal.

Will the crust be as good?

Very close. The Dutch oven is just the most consistent home method.

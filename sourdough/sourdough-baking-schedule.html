A Simple Sourdough Baking Schedule

How to fit a loaf around work and sleep.

By BreadScopeCraft Kitchen·Updated Nov 24, 2026·6 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

Most sourdough schedules follow the same order — feed the starter, mix, bulk ferment, shape, cold proof, bake. The fridge is your friend: a cold proof lets you pause for 8–16 hours and bake when it suits you.

In this guide
  1. 01  A weekend-friendly plan
  2. 02  A weekday plan
  3. 03  Adjusting for temperature
STEP / 01

A weekend-friendly plan

Friday 10 pm: feed your starter. Saturday 8 am: mix the dough. 8:30 am–12:30 pm: stretch-and-folds and bulk ferment. 1 pm: shape and refrigerate.

Sunday morning: bake straight from the fridge.

STEP / 02

A weekday plan

Morning before work: feed the starter with a larger ratio (such as 1:5:5) so it peaks in the evening.

Evening: mix and bulk ferment, shape before bed, refrigerate and bake the next evening.

STEP / 03

Adjusting for temperature

Warmer dough ferments faster. Use cooler water in summer and warmer water in winter to keep the dough around 75–78°F (24–26°C).

If you need more time, put the dough in the fridge to slow it down.

BAKER’S TIP

Write your times down for a few bakes. Your kitchen’s pattern will quickly become clear.

Frequently asked questions

Can I proof longer than 16 hours?

Often up to 24–36 hours in a cold fridge, though the bread becomes more sour and may spread more.

Can I bake the same day?

Yes. After shaping, proof at room temperature 1–3 hours and bake.

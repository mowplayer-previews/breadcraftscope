Beginner Sourdough Bread

A straightforward first loaf with an overnight rest in the fridge.

By BreadScopeCraft Kitchen·Updated Nov 24, 2026·Jump to recipe ↓
Makes1 round loaf
Prep30 min hands-on
Rise4–6 hours + 8–16 hours in the fridge
Bake40–45 min
Oven500°F then 450°F (260°C then 230°C)
WHY IT WORKS

This loaf uses an active starter, stretch-and-folds instead of kneading and a cold overnight proof that fits around a normal day. Timings depend on your kitchen temperature — watch the dough.

Ingredients

  • 450 gbread flour
  • 50 gwhole wheat flour
  • 350 gwater
  • 100 gactive sourdough starter, at its peak
  • 10 gfine salt

Method

  1. Mix the flours with 325 g of the water until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest 30–60 minutes.
  2. Add the starter, salt and remaining 25 g water. Squeeze and fold until fully combined.
  3. Bulk ferment at warm room temperature for 4–6 hours. During the first 2 hours, do 4 sets of stretch-and-folds, 30 minutes apart. The dough is ready when it has grown 50–75%, feels airy and shows bubbles on the sides.
  4. Turn out the dough, shape it into a tight round and place it seam-side up in a floured basket or a bowl lined with a floured towel.
  5. Cover and refrigerate 8–16 hours.
  6. Preheat the oven with a Dutch oven inside to 500°F (260°C) for 45 minutes. Turn the dough onto parchment, score the top with a sharp blade and lower it into the pot.
  7. Bake covered 20 minutes, then lower to 450°F (230°C), uncover and bake 20–25 minutes, until deep brown. Cool at least 1–2 hours before slicing.
BAKER’S TIP

Your kitchen temperature matters more than the clock. In a cool kitchen, bulk fermentation can take 7–8 hours.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my sourdough flat?

Usually under-fermented (bulk too short) or over-fermented. Check rise and bubbles rather than relying on time.

Why so dark?

A deep, caramelized crust adds flavor. If it’s getting too dark, lower the uncovered temperature slightly.

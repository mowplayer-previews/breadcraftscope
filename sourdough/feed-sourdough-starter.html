How to Feed and Maintain a Sourdough Starter

Ratios, schedules and storing it in the fridge.

By BreadCraftScope Kitchen·Updated Nov 24, 2026·6 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

Feeding means discarding some starter and adding fresh flour and water. A common ratio is 1:1:1 by weight — equal parts starter, flour and water. Keep it at room temperature if you bake often, or in the fridge with a weekly feeding if you don’t.

In this guide
  1. 01  Choosing a ratio
  2. 02  Room temperature or fridge
  3. 03  Signs of a healthy starter
STEP / 01

Choosing a ratio

1:1:1 (for example 50 g starter, 50 g flour, 50 g water) peaks in about 4–6 hours at warm room temperature.

A bigger feed, such as 1:5:5, slows things down — useful if you want the starter to peak overnight or while you’re at work.

STEP / 02

Room temperature or fridge

At room temperature, feed once or twice a day.

In the fridge, feed about once a week. Before baking, take it out and give it one or two feedings at room temperature until it’s rising predictably again.

STEP / 03

Signs of a healthy starter

It rises and falls on a regular schedule, smells tangy but pleasant, and has bubbles throughout.

A sluggish starter usually needs warmer conditions or a few regular feedings.

BAKER’S TIP

Use your starter for baking when it’s at or just past its peak — domed, bubbly and roughly doubled.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to discard?

Yes, or the starter grows too large and the flour-to-culture ratio gets too low. Use the discard in crackers or pancakes.

Can I use tap water?

Usually yes. If your water is heavily chlorinated, leave it out for a few hours or use filtered water.
